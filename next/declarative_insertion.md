# Declarative insertion: the model names where to put its own scratch space

> Status: **design note** (2026-09-10) - nothing built, nothing measured.
> The constructive answer to the roadmap's Declarative Attention watch entry
> ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md), 2026-09-06), which parked Ho et al. because the
> byte-latent path has no KV cache to gather from. This note keeps the part
> that survives that objection - a legible action the model emits, with a
> compositional address - and drops the KV-block gather it was attached to.
> Siblings: [field_query.md](field_query.md) (whose "slot vs coordinate"
> argument is reused verbatim below), [forced_computation.md](forced_computation.md)
> (capacity paid in compute), [ouroboros.md](ouroboros.md) and the KL halting
> thread (what the model can already choose today).

## The proposal, as stated

The model emits a coordinate list as literal text - `<think>4c</think>`, and by
composition `<think>4c,6j,7u</think>`. A parser reads it and, during the
forward pass, **inserts an extra sequence position** at recurrent depth 4
holding the learned vector `T[4, 'c']` from a 2D bank indexed by
(depth row, slot letter).

The claim that makes it interesting: this needs **no finetuning corpus**.
Sample coordinate lists at random during pretraining, perform the insertion,
and write the same list into the input as text. The tag and its effect are
generated from one draw, so the association is supervised by construction.

*(Reading of the notation assumed throughout: `4` is the depth row, `c` is the
third entry of that row. If `c` was meant as something else, most of the
analysis below still holds but section "The address is dead on arrival" is the
part that changes.)*

## What is actually free, and what is not

The scheme has two halves and the naive version delivers exactly one.

**Grounding is free, and this is a real result.** Random-coordinate training
does teach the model what `4c` *means*: it can predict the bytes that follow a
`4c` tag, because the insertion genuinely changed them. That is self-supervised
semantics for an action space, with no corpus. Keep it.

**Policy is not free.** The coordinate list came from the RNG, so cross-entropy
on those bytes teaches the model to predict a uniform random string. Nothing
anywhere says `4c` was a *better* choice than `4j` at this position. The
insertion sits on the differentiable path; the emission is a discrete choice
with no reward attached. At inference the model reproduces the sampler.

This is the standard place these schemes die, and it is worth naming the three
known exits rather than rediscovering them:

- Coconut (continuous thought) avoids it by having no discrete emission at all,
  and pays for that with no legibility and no composition.
- Quiet-STaR attaches REINFORCE against downstream likelihood improvement.
- Toolformer filters: sample candidate calls, keep the ones that measurably
  reduce loss on the following tokens, train on the survivors.

## The repair that keeps "no human data"

Toolformer's filter, which is unusually cheap here because the "tool call" is a
forward pass that already runs.

On a cadence (not every step - this is the tasker-style sampling shape, not a
per-batch cost): draw `M` candidate coordinate lists for a sample, run the
forward under each, keep the one minimizing LM loss on the bytes **after** the
tag, and write that list into the text as the emission target. The tag now has
a target generated by measured benefit rather than by an RNG. Still zero human
data. Expert iteration on an action space the model invented the vocabulary for.

Cost is `M` forwards on the sampled fraction. `M = 2` is enough to start - the
question at first is only whether a *chosen* coordinate beats a *random* one,
and that is a two-arm comparison.

## The address is dead on arrival (the symmetry problem)

`T[4,'c']` and `T[4,'j']` begin as two random vectors appearing in
statistically identical contexts. Under random-coordinate training they
converge to the same vector - the mean useful scratch direction - and the
letter carries zero bits. This is slot collapse, structurally the same failure
as VQ dead codes (which this encoder already fights, `vq_reset_codes` /
`vq_stale_after`) and the same shape as the flat-signature collapse the serpent
cell instruments.

Chicken-and-egg: without a policy the slots are interchangeable, and with
interchangeable slots the policy has nothing to climb. The repair above breaks
it from the policy side. Three cheaper reframes break it from the address side,
and at least one of them should be tried first.

### 1. Point at the model's own sequence, not a private bank

`<think>4c</think>` = "at depth 4, insert a copy of latent #3 **from my own
sequence**." The address names a real thing on step one, the symmetry is broken
by content rather than by training, it costs zero new parameters, and it is a
*gather* - which the roadmap entry already identified as the only part of
Declarative Attention worth keeping (a gather skips work; a dense `-inf` mask
costs more than it saves).

This is the version closest to the original paper, and it is the version this
note recommends probing first.

### 2. Fractional depth, not a depth index

Depth is not a stable axis in this decoder. `LOOP_PRIORS` (`praxis/halting/kl.py:38`)
under `kl_log` at budget 18 gives mode 3 / mean 3.88 training loops, while
`get_depth()` (`kl.py:145`) returns the **full** depth at inference with KL
convergence as the only brake. Consequences:

- An emitted `4` names a loop that frequently never executes. Supervision is
  sparse, and inconsistently sparse.
- It is self-referential: an insertion at depth 2 perturbs the states the
  depth-3 halting check reads (`sequential.py:182`), so the insertion changes
  whether depth 4 is reached at all.
- Train/inference mismatch runs the wrong way - `12q` is nearly untrained but
  perfectly reachable at eval.

`<think>0.4c</think>` - 40% of the way through whatever loop actually ran - is
well-defined under every halting draw and composes with the sampler instead of
fighting it. This is [field_query.md](field_query.md)'s argument transferred
without modification: *depth is a slot, make it a coordinate*, and queries
outside the trained range become well-formed rather than undefined.

### 3. A fixed slot budget, so shapes stay static

Insert exactly `N` positions at every depth, with a null vector among the
selectable choices. The model still chooses; the tensor never changes shape.

Variable-length insertion has to reconcile `compressor.reduce_sequence`
(`sequential.py:200`), `expand_sequence(hidden_states, seq_len)`
(`sequential.py:239`), `block_ids`, `positions`, the attention mask, and cache
slots keyed by `current_depth` (`praxis/attention/cache.py:24`) - and it hands
`torch.compile` a per-batch-element dynamic shape, which this stack has already
paid for once. Mixture-of-Depths' capacity trick applies directly.

## The obstacle specific to this stack

The tag is bytes. The insertion is in trunk latent space, two transforms away.

`AbstractinatorEncoder` patches at `target_compression_ratio: 0.125`
(`praxis/encoders/abstractinator/encoder.py:49`) and pushes the result through
a **lossy residual VQ** before the trunk sees anything. So the coordinate
string can be quantized away: the trunk may never receive `4c` as a distinct
symbol, only as whatever fraction of a code vector it occupies. And the round
trip has to close in both directions - the byte decoder must emit those exact
bytes, and the encoder must read them back.

The fix is to parse the tag out at the byte level, before patching, as a
control channel. That works, but it is worth being honest that it costs the
plan its elegance: "it's just text, so it's free" becomes "it's a parser," which
is precisely the inference-engine machinery Ho et al. build.

## What this buys over simply not halting

The question to be able to answer before writing code, since the trunk is
weight-shared and recurrent and can already spend more compute by declining to
halt.

**Width, not depth.** More loops give more transformations of the same state.
An inserted position gives more *state*: a scratch slot that persists across
depths and is attendable from every position. That is a different resource, and
it is the one the halting mechanism cannot produce.

The strongest argument for trying it *here specifically* follows from that
framing. Darcet et al. found ViTs spontaneously hijack low-information
background patches as scratch registers, and that supplying dedicated ones
fixes the resulting artifacts. This trunk sees 33-257 latents, every one of
them carrying compressed content through a VQ bottleneck. The "no spare
position available to repurpose as scratch" condition is *more* likely to hold
here than in a long-context token model. That is a prediction, and it is
falsifiable: if the model has spare capacity in its latents, dedicated
registers should do nothing.

## What it likely costs

Stated in advance so a bad result is not reinterpreted afterwards.

**The emission will probably be ignored at first.** A model that gains nothing
from the slot learns to emit a constant tag, and a model that gains something
diffuse learns to emit the marginal. Instrument for it directly: entropy of the
emitted coordinate distribution, and mutual information between the emitted
coordinate and anything about the input. A flat coordinate distribution is the
failure mode, and it is the same reading as `activation_mix_entropy`.

**It may only reproduce the pause-token result.** If the gain is "an extra
position helps" and not "*choosing* the position helps," then the whole
declarative apparatus is decoration on top of Goyal et al. The null arm that
separates these is **random coordinates at inference** - same insertion budget,
no choice. If chosen ties random, the address is not doing work.

**The parser is real surface area.** Byte-level extraction pre-patching, a
budget clamp, and a decode-time path that has to fire mid-generation.

**It cannot be bolted onto an existing checkpoint** - see below.

## Prior art

- **Pause tokens** (Goyal et al. 2024, [arXiv:2310.02226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02226)).
  Learnable `<pause>` tokens on a fixed schedule. The finding that matters
  here: gains required pretraining **with** the tokens, and finetuning-only
  injection was neutral-to-harmful. That is the closest measured analogue to
  "no finetuning data at all," and it says the insertion must be in the
  pretraining distribution from step 0. Fine for a fresh arm; fatal for
  bolting this onto `-u`.
- **Registers** (Darcet et al. 2024, [arXiv:2309.16588](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16588)).
  The width argument, with evidence, plus the "no spare position" condition.
- **Coconut** (Hao et al. 2024, [arXiv:2412.06769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06769)).
  The user's own comparison. Continuous thought fed back as input: no discrete
  emission, so no credit-assignment problem, and no legibility either.
- **Quiet-STaR** (Zelikman et al. 2024, [arXiv:2403.09629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09629)).
  The REINFORCE repair for exactly this credit assignment gap.
- **Toolformer** (Schick et al. 2023, [arXiv:2302.04761](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)).
  The self-supervised filter recipe this note's repair is lifted from.
- **Memory Transformer** (Burtsev et al. 2020, [arXiv:2006.11527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11527))
  and prefix/prompt tuning (Li & Liang 2021, [arXiv:2101.00190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00190);
  Lester et al. 2021, [arXiv:2104.08691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08691)).
  The bank-of-learned-vectors ancestor, all with harness-chosen placement.
- **Mixture-of-Depths** (Raposo et al. 2024, [arXiv:2404.02258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02258)).
  The static-shape capacity trick for reframe 3.
- **Declarative Attention** (Ho et al. 2026, [arXiv:2609.02737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02737))
  and its trained ancestor, Self-Selected Attention Span (Jin et al. 2024,
  [arXiv:2404.09336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09336)). The origin, per the
  roadmap entry.

Nothing found that lets a model **name the position of its own scratch slot**.
Placement is a harness decision in every entry above; the closest thing to a
model-chosen address is Ho et al., and there the address points at KV blocks
rather than at an insertion site. That gap is the contribution if this works.

*(arXiv IDs above are from memory and should be spot-checked before any of them
reach `research/`.)*

## Cheapest first probe

Skip the bank, skip the parser, skip the emission. Test the **premise** first,
because everything else is downstream of it:

1. Insert `N` learned register vectors at a **fixed** depth, harness-chosen, no
   tag and no text. Pretrain a small arm with them. This is Goyal/Darcet
   replicated on this trunk and it answers "does an extra latent position help
   a 33-257-latent recurrent trunk at all." If it does not, stop - every
   version of this note is dead and it cost one run.
2. If it helps: **random** coordinates, harness-chosen but varying (which
   depth, which slot), still no text. Does varying placement beat fixed
   placement? This measures whether the address space has any structure worth
   addressing, still with no policy and no parser.
3. Only then the tag, the parser, and the best-of-`M` self-labeling loop - with
   random-coordinates-at-inference as the standing null arm.

Step 1 is a config change and a small module. Steps 2 and 3 are the research.

## Open questions

- Does the insertion interact pathologically with KL halting? An extra position
  changes the distribution the convergence check reads, so the model could
  learn to insert **in order to avoid halting**, buying depth through a
  mechanism meant to buy width. Worth watching in the Halting Distribution as
  a function of insert count.
- Should the inserted slot survive to the next depth, or be re-inserted fresh
  each time? Persisting makes it a register; re-inserting makes it a per-depth
  bias. The first is the interesting one and the harder one to plumb through
  `reduce_sequence`.
- What does `expand_sequence(hidden_states, seq_len)` do with the extra
  positions - drop them, or is their content wanted by the byte decoder? Drop
  is the obvious first answer. "The scratch slot is readable at decode" is a
  much larger design.
- Does the composition claim (`4c,6j,7u`) actually hold, or does the second
  coordinate's usefulness depend on the first in a way one draw cannot teach?
  The random sampler generates combinations, so it is at least *exposed* to
  them; whether the model generalizes to unseen combinations is the test that
  makes "compositional address" more than a description of the syntax.
